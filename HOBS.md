# Hobs

The reference for the art, the words and the prompts (SPEC-v3.5.8 §1).

> **Hobs** are small household spirits. In old English folklore a hob lives in a house, does its work quietly, and is
> fed and treated kindly by the people who live there. These hobs:
> - come from eggs that turn up in the nursery, or that a grown hob lays;
> - live about one season: hatchling, little one, young one, grown, old one;
> - eat what grows in the house and what the visitor leaves;
> - need rest, comfort, company and something new to think about;
> - think with their paws: making is how a hob works things out;
> - belong to a family, and inherit their parent's body, temper, words, beliefs and things;
> - live alongside the Works, the house's machine, and the visitor, who comes and goes.
>
> A hob is "a hob", several are "hobs", and the families are "the first family's hobs" and so on.

- **On the page and in what hobs are told,** they are hobs ("you are a hob"). Code, routes and file names keep "pet".
- **Names** are real names (names.toml, engine/names.py): five offered when a hob names an egg, from the screens, the
  name bank, the family's sound, an old family name, and something from its own life. A phrase made of the family's
  words is a **by-name** ("Clementine, called Open Hand"), never a name.
- **Things** are called what they are: a kind and one telling word ("felt cushion", "clay tile"). A hob's own name
  for a thing is its **title**, shown for firsts, inventions, heirlooms, statues, gifts and starred things.
